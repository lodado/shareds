# Interaction model

## Mood state

- `valence` −1…1 (unhappy ↔ content), `arousal` 0…1 (calm ↔ alert), `energy` 0…1 (sleepy ↔ playful),
  `tolerance` 0…1 (petting budget). Personality (boldness, sociability, playfulness) scales the thresholds.
- Move smoothly: valence and arousal change at most 1.5 per second unless a stimulus just happened, and drift
  back to the personality baseline over tens of seconds. Energy falls slowly while awake, faster in play, and
  refills in sleep.

## Appraisal: what is this input?

- **Hand**: a slow approach from the side or below, at the cat's level → sniff, then rub or ignore. Fast, from
  above, or straight at the face → wary.
- **Prey**: small, near the floor, moving erratically, across or away from the cat, hiding and reappearing →
  watch, stalk, pounce. Coming straight at the cat or too fast to follow → watch only, or back off.
- **Threat**: a sudden loud sound, a sudden touch (poke), something looming → startle.
- **Social cue**: a name call, a slow blink, a person crouching → an ear turn, maybe an approach or a blink.

## Petting

- Zone drain per stroke: cheeks, between eyes and ears, chin = low; head top, shoulders, back = medium; flank =
  higher; tail base and belly = high, and the first stroke there already draws a tail flick or an ear turn.
- A stroke is one pass along the fur. Three or four strokes and a pause keep the budget; long continuous
  petting drains it even on good zones.
- While tolerance is high: lean in, narrow the eyes, purr after two or three strokes, maybe knead or rub.
- Below a threshold: warnings in escalating order ([body language](body-language.md)). Stopping ends them.
- At the bottom, if strokes continue: a swat (claws in, for a game), then leave and groom.
- Tolerance refills over tens of seconds without touch; the cat may come back and rub to ask for more.

## Play

- watch → stalk (if the toy is far) → crouch with wiggle → pounce → bat or pin → again, or lose interest. The
  pounce fires when the toy pauses or moves away inside range.
- Energy falls with each pounce; low energy ends play (sit, groom, lie down). A toy that vanishes is watched at
  its last spot for a few seconds.
- Misses are part of play; after one the cat may groom briefly before trying again.

## Startle, habituation, sensitization

- A sudden poke or noise → startle within 0.12 s, then wary or flee depending on strength.
- Keep a decaying count per stimulus kind: harmless repeats weaken the reaction; repeated pokes raise irritation
  and end in a swat.

## Calls and slow blinks

- Name call: the ear nearest the source turns, then maybe the head; the cat comes only when content, awake and
  not busy. Ignoring is the most common answer.
- A slow blink from the person: a relaxed cat may answer with half-blinks and a long narrowing; answering makes
  an approach afterwards more likely.

## Never

- One fixed, instant response per input (the cat becomes a button).
- A swat or bite with no visible warning first.
- A mood that flips with no transition.
- Every zone rewarded the same way.
- Perfect loops: identical durations, both ears in sync, blinks on a beat.
