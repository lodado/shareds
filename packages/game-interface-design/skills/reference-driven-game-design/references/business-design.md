# Business as a falsifiable hypothesis

Describe target use occasion, discovery route, immediate value, replay, return and optional deeper engagement. Competitor revenue, virality and platform availability need actual dated sources; do not turn a famous example into a forecast.

For a first small game suggest at most one low-cost meta-system, or explain why none is needed. State its player benefit, creation/maintenance cost and impact on the core loop. Avoid daily obligations and economy complexity by default.

Choose one distribution candidate before specifying SDK work. At execution time check its official policies for ads, IAP, external navigation, exclusivity, platform lifecycle, age/content and payments. Record URL/date/version and unknowns. No platform terms or percentages are hardcoded here.

Optional rewarded ads: user opt-in, specific reward, reject path, non-ad replay, limit assumption, cancel/failure/duplicate handling, pause/resume and assisted-score policy. No hidden difficulty adjustment to sell rescue; no fake close buttons, accidental clicks or fabricated scarcity.

For revenue estimates use delivered monetizable impressions, not requests. Define currency, time period, gross/net basis, eCPM, revenue share and costs. A developer-net eCPM must not be multiplied by the same share twice. Label every number sourced or assumed. Show sensitivity and costs rather than one confident income figure.

Business experiment: identify funnel stage, cohort, observable event, falsifiable expectation, smallest test and next decision. Acquisition spending is not justified until a testable contribution-margin hypothesis exists. Do not claim statistical or commercial validity from a handful of usability sessions.

Privacy, minors, real payment and competitive prizes are explicit policy dependencies. Record required review instead of inventing a legal clearance. This planning skill does not enroll accounts, publish a game or accept terms.
